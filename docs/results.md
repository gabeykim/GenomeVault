## E1 · Red team: can an attacker who sees all outbound traffic recover the real question?

![red team](charts/redteam.svg)

500 synthetic patients, 3 asks each. Real topic guessed:

| Condition | Repeat-ask attacker | Statistical attacker | Condition area guessed | A real med inferred | Genotype leaked |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Naive (full question + profile) | 100% | 100% | 100% | 100% | 100% |
| Generalized + 7 random decoys, fresh each ask | 95% | 97% | 97% | 97% | 0% |
| Random decoys, asked only once | 13% | 24% | 42% | 34% | 0% |
| Generalized + fixed bucket of 8 (GenomeVault) | 9% | 17% | 36% | 23% | 0% |

Baselines: 1 in 8 = 12.5%; guessing the most common clinical area with no traffic = 34%.

**Reading it.** Naive leaks everything. Random decoys look fine once (13%) but collapse when a question repeats, because only the real topic survives every ask. Fixed buckets send the same 8 every time, so repetition reveals nothing: the repeat-ask attacker sits near 1 in 8 (9%). The **statistical attacker** is the honest number: by betting on the most common topic in the bucket it reaches 17%. The partition's ceiling for that attacker is 16%; it falls to 12.5% only when topics in a bucket are equally common, which is a bucket-design target for B. Genotypes, ages and lab values never leave in generalized topics.

### Joint inference: do simultaneous buckets reveal the condition?

![joint inference](charts/joint.svg)

| Sending pattern | Condition guessed | Real topics recovered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| All buckets in one session | 60% | 44% |
| Separate sessions + 2 cover buckets each, same device (linkable) | 26% | 7% |
| Separate sessions + cover, unlinkable (needs relay) | 24% | 3% |

Baseline: guessing the most common condition = 19% (13 synthetic conditions). The attacker is Bayesian and knows the public cover rate.

**Reading it.** Sending a discharge bundle in one session lets an attacker name the condition about 3.1x more often than a blind guess (D-006). Separate sessions plus cover buckets bring it close to the blind guess. Linking by IP still helps the attacker a little, which is why the relay layer stays on the roadmap (README limitations).

## E3 · Latency, cloud usage and coverage

![latency and cost](charts/latency_cost.svg)

| Mode | Median latency | Cloud calls | Cloud tokens | Personal fields sent | Coverage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Local only | 925 ms | 0 | 0 | 0 | 60% |
| Cloud only (naive) | 3000 ms | 20 | 17,000 | 420 | 100% |
| Hybrid (GenomeVault) | 935 ms | 6 | 15,780 | 0 | 100% |

Latency and tokens are **modelled** (constants: {"rules": 5, "kb": 20, "local_llm": 900, "cloud_bucket": 2500, "cloud_full": 3000}, tokens {"bucket_in": 630, "bucket_out": 2000, "full_in": 450, "full_out": 400}, 1440 bytes per bucket). Coverage is the share of questions reaching their expected answer state, not graded quality: pending live judge (coverage reported instead). Note the honest trade-off: hybrid makes far fewer calls, but each bucket carries 8 questions, so token savings are smaller than call savings.

## Method and limits

- Synthetic priors, domains and clusters: `eval/fixtures/universe_v1.json` (illustrative, not real prescription data).
- Attackers see only outbound traffic plus public knowledge (partition, priors, clusters, cover rate).
- Network metadata (IP, timing, account) is out of scope today; the linkable rows show what it costs.
- Demo, not medical advice.
